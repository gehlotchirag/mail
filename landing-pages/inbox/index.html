FAQ

Common questions

Can I use my own domain name?

Yes — that's the whole point. Inbox lets you receive and send email at any domain you own: yourcompany.com, yourstartup.in, anything. We support multiple domains per account, including custom subdomains like mail.yourcompany.com.

If you don't have a domain yet, we can point you to registrars that work well with our DNS setup guide.

Is my data stored in India?

Yes. All mail data — messages, attachments, metadata, and backups — is stored on DigitalOcean servers in the Mumbai (BOM1) region. Your data never leaves India. We do not use overseas cloud CDNs for mail storage or processing.

This makes Inbox a strong choice for businesses subject to India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act.

Can I use Outlook, Apple Mail, or Thunderbird?

Absolutely. Inbox supports standard IMAP and SMTP, so any email client that speaks those protocols works out of the box. We also support JMAP, the newer protocol that gives faster sync and lower bandwidth use in clients that support it.

In the console, we generate app-specific passwords and step-by-step configuration guides for Outlook, Apple Mail, Thunderbird, Android Gmail, and iOS Mail.

How does email migration work?

From the console, connect your existing provider using either OAuth (for Zoho and Google Workspace) or IMAP credentials (for cPanel, Dovecot, or any IMAP server). We pull every mailbox, every folder, and every message in the background.

You can watch live per-mailbox progress. Once import is complete, update your MX records to point at Inbox and your team is live — nothing is lost, nothing is duplicated.

What happens when the 14-day trial ends?

Your account moves to read-only mode — you can still read your email and export your data, but sending is paused. We send reminders at 7 days and 1 day before the trial ends so you're never caught off guard.

Add a payment method in the console at any time during or after the trial to continue without interruption. We accept all major Indian debit and credit cards, UPI, and net banking.
